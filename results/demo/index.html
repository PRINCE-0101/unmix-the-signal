Hear a mixture.
Recover the hidden signals.

Three independent sources are mixed into three observed recordings. A from-scratch FastICA pipeline sees only those mixtures, estimates independent components, and reconstructs the hidden sources. Compare the results by ear, inspect the waveforms, and verify the recovery with correlation and SI-SDR metrics.

Before vs after

The project makes the blind source separation problem easy to explain during a demo or viva.

Before — mixed observations

Each simulated microphone receives a different linear combination of all three hidden sources.

  • Every channel contains overlapping information.
  • The mixing matrix is not given to FastICA.
  • Listening to one mixture does not isolate one source.

After — independent components

After centering, whitening and FastICA, the recovered components closely match the hidden sources up to the standard ICA ambiguities.

  • Order can be permuted.
  • Sign and scale may differ.
  • Correlation and SI-SDR quantify the match.

How the pipeline works

Four steps take the experiment from synthetic source generation to measurable recovery.

1

Generate independent sources

Create speech-like, music-like and percussion-like signals with different statistical behaviour.

2

Mix the signals

Multiply the source matrix by a mixing matrix so every observed channel becomes a different blend.

3

Whiten + FastICA

Center and PCA-whiten the observations, then iteratively maximize non-Gaussianity using a tanh contrast.

4

Match and evaluate

Resolve permutation/sign ambiguity for evaluation and report absolute correlation plus SI-SDR.
